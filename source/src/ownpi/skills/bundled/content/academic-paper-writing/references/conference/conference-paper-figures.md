# Conference Paper Figures and Tables

This document provides high-level guidance for planning figures and tables. For detailed production instructions, consult the relevant figure-generation skills.

## Figures

- Typically, use one figure to introduce the overall workflow and one figure to present the specific method proposed in the paper.

- For experimental results, use figures when distributions, trends, qualitative behavior, failure modes, mechanisms, or complex relationships need to be shown. Choose the representation that best communicates the evidence; do not force a table-centered presentation or add figures without an argumentative role.

## Tables

- Typically, present the main experimental results, especially performance comparisons, in tables. Use tables for precise quantitative comparisons across datasets, tasks, metrics, or settings, as well as for ablations and efficiency results. Give each table a clear comparison purpose, and keep baselines, settings, and metrics fair and interpretable.
