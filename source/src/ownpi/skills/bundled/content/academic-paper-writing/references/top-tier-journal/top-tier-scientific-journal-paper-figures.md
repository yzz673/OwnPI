# Top-Tier Scientific Journal Paper Figures and Tables

This document provides high-level guidance for planning figures and tables. For detailed production instructions, consult the relevant figure-generation skills.

## Figures

- The main text usually contains four to six multi-panel figures. In the usual manuscript structure, use the first main-text figure to present the framework of the complete work. Main-text figures must not be overly simple, such as figures containing only two or three panels, or strongly homogeneous, such as figures whose panels all use the same chart type. Prefer figures with sufficient structural complexity, complementary panel roles, and varied visual forms when the evidence supports them. Complexity and variation must remain justified by the scientific content rather than added as decoration.
- Supplementary figures generally extend and complement the main-text figures.

## Tables

- Use tables for compact, exact listings or side-by-side lookup, such as examples, metadata, configurations, or supplementary measurements.
- Avoid main-text tables when a figure communicates the information more clearly. Place supporting metadata, configurations, and extended comparisons in Supplementary materials when they are not central to the main inference.
- Give every table a clear manuscript role, source lineage, consistent units, explicit column definitions, and a self-contained caption.
