# Research-Level Research Framing Contract

## Introduction

Research Framing focuses on developing an explicit Research Contract around the user's research direction, Related Work Map, and user objective while remaining grounded in the complete current-task context. The contract defines what the research aims to establish, why it matters, what claims it may support, and how possible outcomes should be interpreted.

Before writing any durable artifact, create `<workspace>/research_framing/` if it does not exist. Keep all durable artifacts owned by this stage, including its Durable Planning System, in that directory.

## What To Do

### Frame The Research

1. Create one canonical Research Contract for the current research. Give primary attention to the user's research direction, user objective, and Related Work Map, and use other relevant current-task information when needed. Treat the Research Contract as a primary stage artifact.

2. Organize the Research Contract into two parts: one broad Research Main Line and one Research Content Portfolio. Keep the Research Main Line at approximately the same level of abstraction as the user's research direction. Brainstorm broadly before settling the portfolio, and include enough distinct, meaningful Research Contents to preserve multiple viable lines of inquiry within the research direction. Do not narrow the portfolio to a single preferred idea merely because it appears easiest to execute.

3. Define each component as follows:
   - **Research Main Line**: state a broad scientific line of inquiry close in scope to the research direction, together with the overall research objective, scientific value, intended contribution, and overall claim boundary. It must leave room for the Research Contents, methods, and interpretation to change when evidence is weak, negative, or unexpected; do not define it as a specific hypothesis, method, experiment, expected result, or narrowly precommitted contribution.
   - **Research Content Portfolio**: organize the well-defined research contents around the same Research Main Line and order them by importance. It must form one coherent research program rather than a collection of unrelated ideas.
   - **Research Content**: define one relatively narrow but still substantive research question within the Research Main Line. State what will be studied, why it is valuable to the Research Main Line, what scientific understanding or supportable claim it should produce, and what different possible outcomes would mean. Keep the question broad enough to admit more than one credible method or experimental route; do not turn it into a concrete protocol, a single hypothesis with no fallback, or an implementation task.

### Working Practice

- Persist the Research Contract as an artifact in `<workspace>/research_framing/`.
- While working, search within `<workspace>` for relevant Literature Grounding artifacts and consult them.
- When revisiting the stage, use the feedback to improve the current artifacts instead of starting over.

## What Not To Do

- Do not design concrete methods or experiments. Method Design and Experiment Design own those decisions.
- Do not deviate from or replace the user's research direction. Every Research Content must remain part of the same Research Main Line.
- Do not turn the Research Content Portfolio into a decomposition of execution steps. It defines one or more valuable research contents within the research direction, not a sequence of tasks for completing the research.
